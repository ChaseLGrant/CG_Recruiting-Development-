Recruiting Advising

Navigate the recruiting process with a strategy—not a guess. We help you target the right schools, reach the right coaches, and manage the timeline.

The Recruiting Process Is Broken

Most families approach recruiting the wrong way. They wait too long, contact the wrong schools, send generic emails, and miss key windows. The rules are confusing, timelines are tight, and the information out there is often outdated or misleading.

We fix that. Our recruiting advising gives you a clear, strategic plan—based on where you are, where you want to go, and what coaches actually look for.

The Recruiting Timeline

Recruiting happens earlier than most families expect. Here's what should be happening at each stage.

Freshman / Sophomore

Build Your Foundation

Focus on development. Get your metrics baseline. Start building a target list of schools. Create your social media presence and basic highlight content. Attend local camps and showcases to get exposure.

Sophomore Summer

Initial Outreach Begins

Begin reaching out to coaches at your target schools. Send introductory emails with your profile, schedule, and video. Attend prospect camps at schools you're interested in. Coaches can now respond—so be ready.

Junior Year

Peak Recruiting Window

This is the most critical period. Consistent communication with coaches. Unofficial visits. Updated video and metrics. Camps and showcases. Most D1 and D2 commitments happen during or right after junior year—don't fall behind.

Senior Year

Finalize Your Decision

Narrow your list. Official visits. Compare offers (athletic + academic). Make your commitment. For athletes still looking, D3 and NAIA schools recruit later—don't count yourself out.

Common Recruiting Mistakes

Most families make these errors. Avoid them.

Waiting Too Long to Start

Recruiting moves fast. If you wait until junior year to start outreach, you're already behind. The earlier you begin building relationships, the better.

Only Targeting "Dream Schools"

Everyone wants to play D1. But most athletes end up at D2, D3, NAIA, or JUCO—and have incredible experiences. Build a balanced list.

Sending Generic Emails

Coaches can spot a copy-paste email instantly. Personalize every message. Mention the program, the school, why you're interested.

Not Following Up

Coaches are busy. One email isn't enough. Follow up politely and consistently—after camps, after tournaments, after updates to your profile.

Ignoring Academics

Your GPA and test scores matter—especially at D3 and academic schools. Don't let poor grades eliminate options before you even get seen.

No Video or Bad Video

Coaches want to see you play before they invest time. Highlight videos should be short, well-edited, and showcase real game footage.

The Coach-Friendly Player Profile

Coaches don't have time to dig for information. A great player profile puts everything they need in one place—quickly and clearly.

We help you build a profile that includes:

Key metrics (velo, exit speed, times)
Academic info (GPA, test scores, intended major)
Schedule (travel team, high school, showcases)
Highlight video link
Contact info (player, parent, coach references)
Social media links
